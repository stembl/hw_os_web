14. What Every Tier Wants (Exec to Supplier)

Tuesday, 12:20 p.m., the weekly review stalls because each tier reads a different "current truth" artifact.

The executive asks for confidence in the decision packet; engineering asks for protected technical closure time; PM asks for stable inputs and early risk flags; the supplier asks for one released package with one owner.

Each ask is legitimate on its own, and friction starts when those asks stay implicit and compete through mismatched artifacts.

Alignment fails at interface contracts

Most cross-tier conflict is not ideology. It is interface-contract ambiguity:

  • what each tier needs to make a decision,
  • when that input is needed,
  • which quality bar applies to the artifact.

When a named owner publishes these contracts in one shared artifact, blame loops drop quickly.

What each tier usually needs

Keep each tier contract plain, operational, and tied to one decision artifact.

Executive

  • leading indicators of risk and dependency,
  • clear decision asks,
  • tradeoff framing when date/cost/scope conflict.

PM

  • stable owner map,
  • decision-ready updates,
  • early signal when assumptions break.

Engineering lead

  • clear requirement/risk state,
  • authority boundaries,
  • protected closure windows for technical decisions.

Manufacturing/operations

  • unambiguous release package,
  • process-critical characteristics,
  • change notice with impact timing.

Supplier

  • one current revision source,
  • expected evidence and acceptance criteria,
  • single owner for technical/commercial closure path.

Reciprocal obligations

Needs without reciprocal obligations become entitlement and eventually gridlock.

Pair each ask with an owed output artifact:

  • Exec asks for confidence -> owes timely trade decisions in the decision log.
  • PM asks for predictability -> owes transparent escalation and dependency hygiene in the one-page update.
  • Engineering asks for room to solve -> owes explicit risk state and closure dates in requirement and risk records.
  • Supplier asks for clarity -> owes capability evidence and issue response cadence in the release package.

This framing keeps fairness visible by balancing each tier's ask with a concrete, auditable obligation.

PM fairness, explicitly

When record quality is low, PM becomes the human sync engine compensating for missing system behavior in formal artifacts.

With clean ownership, requirement truth, and one-page integrity, PM work shifts from chasing contradictions to clearing real blockers.

That is the contract to protect: an efficient PM, backed by a process that does not waste people's time, is a PM teams trust and respect.

One meeting format that surfaces gaps early

Run a short cross-tier interface review against one shared contract sheet:

  1. confirm what each tier needs this week,
  2. verify what each tier received,
  3. identify where contract quality failed,
  4. assign which owner closes the gap by when.

Skip long debate; focus on interface quality, owner assignment, and dated closure commitments.

Escalation becomes cleaner

When tier contracts are explicit, escalation becomes factual and auditable:

  • identify which contract failed,
  • state what decision is blocked,
  • quantify current risk and date impact,
  • assign who must decide by when.

That path resolves blockers faster than personality-driven escalation because decision authority and artifact gaps are explicit.
